# Panama generics project: overview and what we need from the factory

*Shared with all partners, including Rutul Patel (Zywie Worldwide). Prepared by the Panama partners, 9 October 2026. All facts below come from public Panamanian sources listed at the end; nothing here is a commitment or a quote.*

## 1. Purpose and status

We are evaluating a Panamanian company that would import generic medicines made by the factory and sell them to Panama's public health system. Before anyone incorporates or spends money, both sides need the same facts: how the buyers work, what registration requires, and what data and documents only the factory can provide. This document puts all of that in one place. **Section 5 is the part for the factory's export and regulatory teams.**

Current status: the Panama side has mapped legal, registration and tax costs. Warehouse, freight and insurance quotes are waiting for the factory data in section 5. Before incorporating, the Panama partners will also obtain a legal opinion on public-procurement eligibility and confirm with the regulator how the warehouse will be licensed.

## 2. How Panama's public sector buys medicines

| Topic | Rule | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Buyers | The Social Security Fund (CSS) and the Ministry of Health (MINSA), plus hospitals and boards that buy smaller lots | [1][2] |
| Main channel | The CSS runs large single-price tenders (LPU). The current one covers 15 months, from 11 February 2026 to 11 May 2027; contracts are at a fixed unit price with a guaranteed minimum purchase of 50% | [3][4][11] |
| Other channels | MINSA reverse auctions for fixed quantities; small purchases (up to B/.70,000 per order for medicines); emergency purchases for products on the critical-shortage list | [1][2][5][6] |
| Who wins | The lowest price that complies with every requirement | [3] |
| Registration | A Panamanian sanitary registration and a distributor licence are required when the offer is submitted; they cannot be fixed afterwards | [3] |
| Manufacturer's letter | A manufacturer's joint-liability letter, apostilled, for each tender | [3] |
| Packaging | Printed at the factory with the tender number (and "Property of MINSA" in MINSA auctions); relabelling or over-labelling is not accepted | [3][7] |
| Shelf life | At least 18 months of shelf life left at delivery | [3] |
| Bonds | Bid bond of 10% (lines above B/.70,000) and performance bond of 30% of the contract in CSS tenders; 20% in MINSA auctions | [3][7] |
| Payment | 90 days after the invoice is accepted and endorsed by the Comptroller; in practice about 150 days from delivery | [4] |
| Liquid medicines | A diethylene and ethylene glycol test certificate for every batch of syrups and suspensions | [8] |

Currency: the balboa (B/.) trades one to one with the US dollar.

## 4. Licensing and registration path

| Step | What it involves | Time (legal limit or our planning estimate) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Company and licence | Panamanian company; distributor licence from the National Directorate of Pharmacy and Drugs (DNFD), with a pharmacist in charge and a licensed warehouse | About 3 months (estimate) |
| Registration dossier | One file per product, filed with the DNFD | Ready when the factory's documents arrive |
| Laboratory analysis | Local quality-control analysis of samples by the reference laboratory before registration (India is not on the WHO list that gives Panama's fast route) | Legal limits: 60 working days for the laboratory and 20 for the DNFD |
| Abbreviated route | No local analysis if the product is registered and sold in Argentina, Brazil, Chile, Colombia, Cuba or Mexico | Shorter; please tell us if any product qualifies |
| Validity | Five years, with two post-registration analyses in that period | — |
| Supplier registration | National register of bidders and CSS supplier registration | 30 working days (legal limit) |

Indian certificates: since 15 August 2025, COPP and WHO-GMP certificates are issued online through ONDLS, and only for products with a valid manufacturing licence [10].

## 5. What we need from the factory

### 5.1 Regulatory package, per product

| Document | Use |
| --- | --- |
| CPP (WHO format) issued through ONDLS, apostilled, with the legal name and address of the manufacturing plant | Registration |
| Current WHO-GMP certificate of the plant and the manufacturing licence for the product | Registration and the tender's manufacturer letter |
| Technical file (CTD module 3): formula, specifications, validated analytical method, stability data for climatic zone IVb, certificates of analysis of three recent batches | Laboratory analysis and registration |
| Packaging artwork and Spanish leaflet; samples and reference standards for the laboratory | Registration and tenders |
| Free-sale certificate and any registrations in other countries | Registration and the abbreviated route |
| Authorisation letter to the Panamanian distributor, apostilled | Registration in the distributor's name |
| Glycol test certificate per batch (syrups and suspensions) | Every import |

### 5.2 Commercial terms

- FOB price per product and presentation, at the minimum order quantity (MOQ) and at three times the MOQ.
- MOQ, approved shelf life, production lead time and the usual payment terms.
- Who invoices (the factory or Zywie), and the factory's usual position on registration costs with its Latin American distributors.

### 5.3 Logistics data, per product

The licensed warehouses, the freight forwarder and the cargo insurer cannot quote without these fields. The factory's standard export packing list and product data sheets usually cover them.

| Field | Example of what is needed |
| --- | --- |
| Product, strength, presentation | Paracetamol 500 mg, blister of 10, box of 100 |
| Units per inner box and per master carton | 100 / 5,000 |
| Master carton dimensions (cm) and gross weight (kg) | 50 × 30 × 30; 12 kg |
| Cartons per pallet and pallet height | 40; 1.6 m |
| Storage conditions | 15-25 °C, protect from light |
| HS code | 3004.xx |
| Shelf life from manufacture | 24 or 36 months |
| Dangerous goods | Not dangerous (MSDS attached) |
| Value per master carton (FOB) | For the cargo insurance |

The first shipment would be small (about 5 pallets, consolidated sea freight to Manzanillo), followed by about 3 to 5 pallets a month once sales start.

### 5.4 Quality

The usual supplier-qualification questionnaire, in writing: regulatory alerts, failed official quality tests or recalls of these products in the last three years and, if any, the root cause and the corrective action. Panamanian regulators can consult the Indian regulator's public lists, so it is best to have this in the file from the start.
